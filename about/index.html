About Me

Software engineer at IBM · Electronic engineering background · Based in the UK

Background

I grew up with a deep curiosity about how things work — from electronics to software to systems. That led me to study electronic engineering, which gave me a solid grounding in hardware, digital logic, and signal processing before I made the move into software full-time.

What makes my path a bit unusual is that I completed my degree while working full-time. It took discipline and time management, but it also taught me how to learn under pressure and deliver when it matters — habits that have served me well in a demanding technical career.

Career at IBM

I joined IBM and have worked across software engineering, testing, and tooling. The majority of my career has been focused on CICS — IBM's battle-tested transaction processing middleware used by banks, airlines, and government systems worldwide. It's the kind of software that has to be right, and working on it has shaped how I think about reliability, correctness, and the value of rigorous testing.

Over the years I've worked on automated test frameworks, build pipelines, developer tooling, and feature delivery. I've contributed to products used at global scale, and I take pride in writing code that lasts.

How I work

  • Simple over cleverCode that's easy to read is easy to maintain. I prefer clear, obvious solutions.
  • Test as you goTesting isn't a phase — it's part of development. I write tests alongside code, not after.
  • Understand the problem firstBefore writing a line of code, I try to fully understand what's being asked — and why.
  • Communicate earlyIf something is unclear, a quick conversation saves hours of rework. I'd rather ask than assume.
  • OwnershipI take responsibility for my work end-to-end: from the first commit to production and beyond.
  • Keep learningTechnology moves fast. I stay current by building things, reading, and being willing to be wrong.

Outside of work

When I'm not in front of a screen, you'll probably find me watching football, obsessing over aviation, or out for a walk with the dog. I've always been fascinated by aircraft — the engineering, the history, the sheer scale of commercial aviation as a system.

I'm also a film person. Science fiction in particular — good sci-fi makes you think about what it means to be human, and the best of it ages remarkably well.
